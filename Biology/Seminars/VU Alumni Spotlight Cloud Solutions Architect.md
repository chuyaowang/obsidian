## Nina's Talk: A Journey of Growth and Curiosity

This section summarizes Nina's main talk, covering her personal and professional journey, key learnings, and advice given to students.

### 1. Personal Story: The Leap from Serbia

* **What she did:** Moved from her home country of Serbia to Amsterdam to study Computer Science at VU Amsterdam. She felt determined to leave her comfort zone but was initially overwhelmed by the new culture, language, and environment.
* **What She Learned (Personal Growth):** The experience of moving alone to a new country taught her that true growth comes from a place of discomfort. She came to see knowledge as a form of personal power and rebellion—something that, once earned, could never be taken away. This mindset, rooted in her upbringing, became a cornerstone of her identity.
* **Advice:**
  * Be **willing** to grow, even if you don't feel **ready**.
  * Embrace challenges as they are opportunities to learn about your own resilience and limitations.

### 2. University Life: Building a Foundation

* **A More Detailed Account:** Nina described her arrival at VU Amsterdam as a culture shock. The academic system, with exams every two months and numerous assignments, was intense. This, combined with being in a new country, led to a period of intense self-doubt where she began to "question her worth." Living on the international campus, which she likened to an "American campus from the movies," was a special experience where she met people from all over the world. The culminating moment of her academic career was her Master's thesis, titled **'radio astronomical feature classification and segmentation: a preliminary study into data-driven reporting'**. This project with the ASTRON radio astronomy center was the first time she took a step back and felt "super proud" of her own work, giving her the confidence to see what was next.
* **What She Learned (Personal Growth):** The initial struggle of comparing herself to her talented peers forced her to make a crucial mental shift. She learned to stop seeking external validation and instead created her own internal definition of success. This was a "game changer" that transformed university from a source of pressure into a personal game she could win on her own terms. Completing her difficult thesis taught her the importance of self-recognition and being kind to herself.
* **Key Skills Gained:**
  * **Academic Discipline:** Mastered an intense academic schedule, developing resilience and time management.
  * **Scientific Research & Analysis:** Gained through her thesis, where she worked with complex physics and astronomical data to classify and segment features.
  * **Fundamental CS Knowledge:** Acquired a deep understanding of the "basics" (algorithms, data structures, systems) that she considers essential to her career.
  * **Cross-Cultural Communication:** Developed by living and studying in a highly international campus environment.
* **Advice:**
  * **Master the basics:** Truly understand the fundamental concepts, as they are the foundation for everything else in a constantly changing field.
  * **Set your own standards:** Define what success means to you and focus on your own path.
  * **Ask for feedback:** Don't be shy. Reach out to professors, TAs, and colleagues. It's a direct sample you can use to grow.
  * **Be proud of your achievements:** Take time to acknowledge your hard work and be kind to yourself.

### 3. The Startup: Building Something from Nothing

* **A More Detailed Account:** The startup was born out of lockdown boredom. A friend from South Africa highlighted a problem: in Cape Town, the same house was being listed with different prices and specs. With "no money" and "no investors," Nina and her friends built a platform in three months that **scraped over 300 real estate agencies**, cleaned the data, and used ML and image recognition to standardize the listings. By showing their prototype to people in the industry, they eventually secured investment.
* **What She Learned (Personal Growth):** This experience taught her that she didn't need a perfect plan or permission to start something. It was a profound lesson in the power of collaborative creativity and turning a "crazy idea" into a tangible, valuable product with a group of friends. She learned to be comfortable with uncertainty and to trust the process of discovery-driven by curiosity.
* **Key Skills Gained:**
  * **Entrepreneurship & Pitching:** Learned how to take a raw idea, build a business case, and present it effectively to secure investment.
  * **Rapid Full-Stack Development:** Gained hands-on experience building a complete system, from data scraping and backend analysis to a user-facing platform.
  * **Product Management:** Managed the entire product lifecycle, from identifying a market need to launching a registered company.
  * **Business Acumen:** Understood that customers buy the *value* a product provides, not just the technology behind it.
* **Advice:**
  * **Just do it:** A few friends with free time and a crazy idea can make something real. Don't wait for the perfect plan.
  * **Let curiosity drive you:** If you are genuinely interested in solving a problem, you will find a way to learn what you need.
  * **Embrace collaboration:** You don't have to be extraordinary by yourself. Great things are built with others.

### 4. Microsoft Part I: Data & AI Consultant (Netherlands)

* **A More Detailed Account:** This was Nina's first corporate role, which she chose over two PhD offers. As a consultant based in the Netherlands, she described her job as being an "engineer that is sold" to clients. She was put on ~53 different projects for major global companies, forcing her to become highly adaptable. A highlight project was for Coca-Cola, where her team created a HoloLens-based "digital twin" of a factory, allowing workers to inspect machine health with 3D overlays.
* **What She Learned (Personal Growth):** This role was a trial by fire in corporate dynamics. Being thrown into high-stakes customer meetings forced her out of her "secure zone" and taught her how to manage her insecurities and project confidence, which she admitted "took a lot of guts." She learned to become comfortable with the "constant unpredictability" of consulting and to take ownership as the face of Microsoft to the client.
* **Key Skills Gained:**
  * **Enterprise Consulting & Stakeholder Management:** Mastered the art of listening to customer problems, managing expectations, and delivering solutions for large, global companies.
  * **Technical Breadth & Adaptability:** Gained rapid experience across a huge portfolio of Microsoft technologies, switching contexts frequently between projects.
  * **Technical Storytelling:** Honed the ability to explain complex technical solutions to customers and demonstrate value.
* **Advice:**
  * **Be adaptable:** In a fast-paced consulting environment, you must be ready for constant change and not be easily overwhelmed.
  * **Develop confidence:** Learn to be comfortable in high-stakes situations and communicate your ideas clearly.
  * **Seek feedback constantly:** Use formal and informal feedback as a crucial tool for survival and growth in a corporate setting.

### 5. Microsoft Part II: Cloud Solutions Architect (UAE)

* **A More Detailed Account:** Nina described this as a "very drastic move" to Abu Dhabi. In this more senior role, her focus has shifted from project implementation to high-level architecture. She now specializes in the "AI and application side of things" and works strategically with the government sector in the UAE on the adoption of "scalable gen AI systems." Her time is split between customer-facing strategy workshops and intensive internal work, presentations, and mentoring.
* **What She Learned (Personal Growth):** This move represents her continued commitment to seeking growth through discomfort. By stepping into a new country and a more senior role, she is learning to operate at a strategic level, shaping policy and AI adoption rather than just executing projects. The role has also formalized her position as a leader and mentor to the next generation of engineers.
* **Key Skills Gained:**
  * **High-Level System Architecture:** Advanced from project-level design to architecting large-scale, scalable GenAI systems for national-level clients.
  * **Strategic Advising:** Developed skills in advising government-level clients on long-term AI strategy and adoption.
  * **Specialized AI Expertise:** Is deepening her knowledge in GenAI and its application in a critical and highly visible sector.
  * **Formal Mentorship:** Has taken on a structured role in mentoring interns across both the Netherlands and UAE offices.
* **Advice:**
  * **Never stop learning:** She emphasized that the need to learn is constant, mentioning she had a new certification exam coming up soon.
  * **Embrace new environments:** Don't be afraid to take on drastic changes, like moving to a new country for a new challenge.
  * **Find opportunities to lead:** As you become more senior, actively look for ways to mentor others and contribute to the community.

### 6. Detailed Q&A Session

Here is a more detailed breakdown of the questions asked by the audience and Nina's responses.

* **Question 1: On the difficult hiring process for AI graduates.**
  * **The Inquiry:** A student with a Master's in AI expressed frustration with the hiring process. They noted that job descriptions often require experience with specific tools (like Azure, Databricks) not covered in university, making them feel that an internship would have been more valuable than their theoretical thesis.
  * **Nina's Answer:** She strongly agreed that **internships are the easiest way in**. However, she advised students not to be discouraged by job descriptions, calling them unrealistic "wish lists" often written by HR or AI. She stressed that companies don't actually expect new graduates to know these specific tools. The interview process will test **core concepts and fundamentals**—like programming assessments and system design questions—which are directly related to university studies. Her key advice was to be proactive: **network on LinkedIn**, find people who work at target companies, and ask them for a brief chat to get real insights into what the company is looking for.

* **Question 2: On whether academia is "dying".**
  * **The Inquiry:** Following up on the internship point, a student asked if the emphasis on practical experience means that academia is losing its value.
  * **Nina's Answer:** She firmly disagreed. She positioned academia as the primary source of **innovation and "cool research,"** citing **OpenAI** as a company built by "peer researchers." She revealed that major companies like Microsoft are **"heavily hiring researchers"** because they are the ones who possess deep knowledge. She believes the most revolutionary changes in technology originate from academic work.

* **Question 3: On the hiring focus on GenAI/LLMs.**
  * **The Inquiry:** A student whose research was in evolutionary computing worried that companies are only interested in hiring for GenAI and LLMs, ignoring other important fields of AI.
  * **Nina's Answer:** She acknowledged that GenAI is the current **"hype"** and naturally dominates job postings. However, she explained that many companies use "AI" as a catch-all term and often don't understand the nuances. The fact that a student *does* understand these different fields is a significant advantage. She pointed out that many specialized roles are _never advertised publicly_, making **networking** the best way to discover them. A candidate's unique profile might be exactly what a company needs, even if they haven't created a formal job posting for it. Sometimes jobs are not listed because the companies do not know what they want. It is important to ask to discover such unlisted opportunities.

* **Question 4: On Microsoft's AI solutions and data privacy.**
  * **The Inquiry:** A student asked about the specifics of Microsoft's AI business, the technology used, and how they handle customer data privacy.
  * **Nina's Answer:** Microsoft's AI business is built on its **cloud infrastructure**. They provide the massive GPU compute power needed to run large models, including those from their partner, OpenAI. The most common business use cases are **document processing and summarization (RAG systems)** and workflow automation. On the critical topic of privacy, she explained the concept of **"data residency."** Microsoft contractually guarantees that a customer's data will remain in a specific geographic region (e.g., a data center within the EU for a European client) to comply with strict data protection laws.

* **Question 5: On what she missed in her academic program.**
  * **The Inquiry:** A professor asked if there was anything missing from her education at VU that would have better prepared her for her career.
  * **Nina's Answer:** Personally, she wishes she had been **less shy and had asked for feedback more often**. Academically, she was very satisfied, noting that her program was up-to-date (e.g., teaching Databricks when it was still niche). Her main struggle was the initial **intensity** of the academic schedule, with frequent exams and deadlines.

* **Question 6: On how her "easy" startup story really happened.**
  * **The Inquiry:** A student asked for the real story behind her startup, which sounded deceptively easy.
  * **Nina's Answer:** She clarified it was a product of circumstance and curiosity. They had ample free time during the pandemic and were driven to solve a real-world problem they had noticed. The process was iterative: they built a prototype and then began **showing it to people in the industry** to ask for feedback. These conversations created a network that eventually led them to investors.

* **Question 7: On finding a mentor as a student.**
  * **The Inquiry:** A student asked how to find a mentor when they don't have a job or corporate experience.
  * **Nina's Answer:** Her advice was simple: **"You can just go up to someone and... ask."** She encouraged students to be brave and proactive. She recommended going to **conferences (many have free student tickets), startup events, and online forums**. When you find someone whose work is inspiring, reach out and ask for a single conversation. It doesn't need to be a formal, long-term commitment from the start.

* **Question 8: On being a woman in a male-dominated field.**
  * **The Inquiry:** A student asked how she handles being a woman in a male-dominated field and deals with prejudice without being perceived negatively.
  * **Nina's Answer:** She admitted it was very difficult at first, leading to significant self-doubt. Her strategy for handling inappropriate comments is to **not engage in the moment**. Instead, she learned to stay quiet and later use the **formal, secure channels** within the corporation to address the issue. She stressed that large companies have processes, hotlines, and mentors to help employees navigate these situations safely and effectively.

* **Question 9: On career regrets.**
  * **The Inquiry:** A student asked if she ever regretted choosing Microsoft over a PhD or leaving her startup.
  * **Nina's Answer:** She has **no regrets**. Her rationale was that she could always return to academia, but the chance to learn within the vast ecosystem of a global company like Microsoft was a unique opportunity. She confirmed she would "for sure" return to the startup world at some point, but for now, her goal is to continue learning in her current role.

### 7. Comprehensive Summary of Nina's Talk

Nina's talk chronicles her journey from a determined student moving from **Serbia to Amsterdam** to her current role as a **Solution Architect at Microsoft in Abu Dhabi**. Her story is one of embracing uncertainty and leveraging it for growth.

She began her journey at **VU Amsterdam**, where she overcame initial struggles by learning to set her own standards for success and mastering the fundamental concepts of computer science. During the pandemic, she co-founded a **real estate startup**, a testament to her belief in learning by doing and the power of collaboration. This experience taught her not only technical skills but also the basics of business and leadership.

Choosing the corporate world, she joined **Microsoft**, where she has thrived in a fast-paced, customer-facing environment, first as a consultant and now as an architect.

**Key lessons from her experience include:**

* **Growth happens in discomfort:** The most significant learning occurs when you step outside your comfort zone.
* **Knowledge is your power:** The fundamentals you learn are timeless and will support you throughout your career.
* **Set your own standards:** Define success for yourself to build confidence and stay motivated.
* **Curiosity and action lead to learning:** The best way to learn is to start building and solving problems.
* **Collaboration and honesty are crucial:** You cannot succeed alone. Build a network, find mentors, and be honest about your struggles.
